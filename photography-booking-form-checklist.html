Photography booking form checklist: 8 fixes for mobile enquiries

Someone likes your photographs. Now they want to ask about a date. Can they submit a useful enquiry on their phone without frustration? Test your own website with these eight checks.

This is a general usability guide, not a promise of increased bookings. Test with your own device and website; do not submit fake leads to other businesses.

1. Put a clear enquiry link beside your work

A wedding gallery should offer a button such as Check wedding availability near the introduction. A portrait gallery can say Ask about a portrait session. Avoid hiding the only contact link below dozens of photographs.

Test: from a mobile gallery, count how many taps it takes to reach the form.

2. Ask only what you need to reply

Start with name, reply email, session type, preferred date (when relevant), and an optional short message. Only require a phone number if there is a clear operational reason. Avoid long questionnaires before the first reply.

Test: fill in the form with one thumb. Note any field you would not want to answer yourself.

3. Explain availability and location

Say what locations you serve, whether you travel, what kinds of sessions you accept and whether a date enquiry is a booking or just a request. This reduces mismatched enquiries.

Test: ask someone unfamiliar with your site whether they can tell what to request.

4. Show a useful confirmation

After submitting, show a plain-language confirmation that says whether the enquiry was received, what happens next, and a realistic reply-time expectation if you can meet it. Do not claim success if delivery failed.

Test: send one clearly labelled test enquiry to your own inbox and check the message actually arrived.

5. Make errors fixable

Keep visible labels, mark required fields clearly, and describe errors beside the affected field. Avoid deleting a whole message when email validation fails.

Test: intentionally miss one required field and inspect the error on mobile.

6. Make the form keyboard- and screen-reader-friendly

Connect labels to inputs, provide visible focus indicators, keep buttons large enough to tap and don't communicate errors with colour alone. Check contrast and form order.

Test: navigate using a keyboard and try your phone's built-in accessibility tools.

7. Respect privacy

Explain what you use enquiry details for and link to your current privacy notice. Collect only the data you need, and check the settings of the form provider and any third-party scripts. Data protection obligations depend on your location and business.

Test: identify where each form submission is stored and who can access it.

8. Retest after one change

Change one obvious source of friction, such as a buried booking link or unnecessary required field. Repeat your test under similar conditions. A faster form is not automatically proof of more bookings.

Test: record the before/after experience without inventing conversion data.
